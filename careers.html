We believe qualifications deserve a closer look.

Background checks & employment barriers

What happens when your background makes finding work harder?

For some people, the hardest part of finding employment isn't the interview or the work itself. It's reaching the background check stage and wondering whether something from their past will end another opportunity.

If you've been denied employment because of a criminal record or have concerns about what a screening report might show, you're still welcome to introduce yourself to Merit & Momentum Recruiting.

Not every job has the same background requirements.

Some positions have restrictions based on licensing, legal requirements, workplace responsibilities, or the type of work involved. Other employers may have more flexibility in how they evaluate applicants.

The relevance of a record may depend on several factors, including the position, the nature of the offense, how much time has passed, and any applicable legal restrictions.

A previous denial doesn't determine every future opportunity.

Being turned down by one employer doesn't necessarily mean every employer will reach the same decision. Hiring standards vary, and candidates may qualify for positions with different responsibilities and screening requirements.

Our goal is to understand your skills and interests while discussing realistic opportunities as employer relationships and confirmed openings develop.

What you can expect from us

You deserve a conversation grounded in facts, not assumptions.

We won't ask you to disclose criminal history through our initial candidate introduction. If an opportunity involves specific background screening requirements, we'll aim to communicate those requirements clearly and discuss relevant next steps with you.

We cannot promise that an employer will overlook a conviction, waive a required screening, or make a particular hiring decision. We can work toward identifying employers and opportunities where a candidate's qualifications receive meaningful consideration.

Second chance employment

Your past is part of your history. Your abilities are part of your future.

We understand that people may have complicated histories, including convictions, employment interruptions, or other circumstances that affect their ability to secure work.

Accountability, personal growth, practical skills, and reliability can all be relevant when someone is prepared to contribute to a workplace. We want to help create informed hiring conversations that consider the person and the position.

Our process

What happens after you introduce yourself?

Complete the candidate introduction with your skills, interests, availability, and general employment history.

We'll consider the information you provide to understand the types of roles that may align with your abilities and goals.
